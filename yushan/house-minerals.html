The only real moat

Two colours nobody else sells

Beitou Sulphur and Yushan Slate are ground from Taiwanese mineral deposits. Neither has a Colour Index equivalent anywhere else. No other paintmaker, in Taiwan or outside it, sells these two colours, because no other paintmaker collects from these two places.

Why a house mineral is different

Every other colour in the range has a Colour Index code you can look up and buy from another maker under another name. These two don't. They're ground stone, not a formulated pigment, and the deposit is the whole reason they exist.

Beitou geothermal valley, Taipei

Beitou Sulphur

Beitou sits at the north edge of Taipei, built around a geothermal valley that has been mined for sulphur and mineral sediment for over a century. The deposit that becomes this pigment forms where mineral-rich steam meets rock, leaving behind a dense, gold-amber sediment with a texture no synthetic pigment can fake.

It is why the colour granulates as hard as it does: the particles are irregular ground mineral, not manufactured pigment crystal, so they settle into the paper's texture unevenly, the same way the mineral settled in the valley itself. There is no Colour Index code for it because there is no formula, only a place.

Yushan range, central Taiwan

Yushan Slate

Yushan, Jade Mountain, is the highest peak in Taiwan and the range this colour is named for. The slate collected from its lower slopes is a cool, close-grained stone that grinds to a fine grey with almost no warmth in it, which is unusual: most mineral greys pull brown or green under a wash. This one doesn't.

It carries zero staining, meaning it lifts almost completely off the paper if you're not happy with a passage, and it granulates very heavily, the mineral separating out into visible texture rather than sitting flat. Like Beitou Sulphur, there's no synthetic equivalent and no Colour Index code, because it isn't a formula either.
